# M6.2 Minimal Risk Decision Design

**Issue:** #35, M6.2 Minimal risk decision (fail-closed)<br>
**Milestone:** M6, Minimal risk, paper execution, ledger and P&L<br>
**Status:** Approved under the user's autonomous-delivery instruction

## Purpose

M6.2 activates the risk-policy authority after M6.1 target construction. Its
pure synchronous evaluator consumes one candidate `TargetPosition` plus an
immutable evidence cut and returns exactly one of:

- a semantic `RiskDecision` whose disposition is approved, modified, or
  rejected;
- a typed fail-closed `RiskObligationUnavailable`; or
- a typed `RiskAdmissionRejected` for a target that never became an accepted
  risk obligation.

This is evaluator return cardinality, not a redefinition of the durable risk
obligation lifecycle. After admission, recoverable `risk.runtime.interrupted`
facts remain non-terminal. An orchestration failure that cannot recover ends in
the Phase 03 incomplete/failed run-scope disposition, not in a fabricated
evaluator return. M6.2 supplies the deterministic semantic function that a
later durable orchestrator resumes or terminates under those rules.

The authority is deliberately permissive but structurally complete. Its only
numeric limit is a versioned maximum absolute projected exposure. It still
requires fresh, compatible account, market, projected-exposure, kill-switch,
publication, mode, and policy evidence before approval. Missing, stale,
recovering, invalid, future, or incompatible evidence never produces an
approval or a fabricated rejection.

M6.2 stops before reservation. It records the projected-exposure identity and
`risk_sequence` it evaluated, but it does not advance that sequence, reserve
capacity, create executable intent, create an order, or write accounting.

## Alternatives Considered

### One aggregate context and one terminal variant (selected)

One call receives the target, policy, deterministic cut, independent
publication/lifecycle facts, and optional typed safety snapshots. Internally it
performs admission,
availability, and semantic evaluation in fixed order. This keeps the public
cardinality explicit while allowing missing evidence to be represented by an
absent optional rather than a forgeable "missing snapshot" object.

### Separate admission and decision APIs

This mirrors the full planning lifecycle more literally, but requires durable
accepted-obligation state and recovery coordination that M6.2 cannot complete
without M6.3 and Phase 03 integration. It is deferred without changing the
terminal contract.

### One decision enum including unavailable

This is smaller, but it contradicts the normative planning contract:
unavailable is not a semantic risk decision. It would also let downstream code
mistake missing evidence for a policy rejection. It is rejected.

## Authority Boundary

`chronos_risk` owns:

- risk admission interpretation;
- risk-policy evaluation;
- approved, modified, and rejected `RiskDecision`s;
- unavailable-obligation and admission-rejection outcomes;
- deterministic decision identity, reasons, rule findings, and modification
  proof.

It may depend only on contracts and the M6.1 portfolio target contract. It may
not depend on reservation, execution planning, adapters, runtime, accounting,
ledger, P&L, UI, telemetry, or storage. It does not issue or edit portfolio
targets.

A new M6 risk-authority gate statically checks includes, sources, owner target,
and allowed links, then configures CMake and asserts the resolved target graph.
The existing portfolio guard remains the final top-level assertion; the risk
guard runs immediately before it after all subdirectories have configured.
Trace sentinels reject skipped guards and post-guard target/source mutations.

## Public Contract

### Identity additions

The shared opaque-ID registry gains:

- `RiskScopeId`;
- `RiskObligationId`;
- `RiskDecisionId`;
- `RiskEvaluationOutcomeId`; and
- `ProjectedExposureId`.

IDs are deterministic SHA-256 projections of complete semantic inputs. The
existing nonzero fallback remains total for every digest.

### Policy and cut

`MinimalRiskPolicy` contains:

- risk-scope ID and exact target key;
- run ID and the one run mode supported by this policy version;
- risk-policy, limit-set, exposure-model, arithmetic, and authority versions;
- the one accepted M6.1 target-schema version;
- exposure scale, `QuantityOnlyV1` exposure-dimension set, and maximum absolute
  projected exposure;
- maximum logical ages for account, market, projected-exposure, and kill-switch
  evidence;
- decision validity duration; and
- whether equal-or-less-risky modification is enabled.

`RiskEvaluationCut` contains the run-input sequence and replay logical time.
Host time and storage order are not inputs.

Policy validation is an operational precondition. Zero durations, negative
ages or limits, invalid modes, a non-`QuantityOnlyV1` dimension set, or
inconsistent scope/version configuration return
`RiskEvaluationFailure::InvalidPolicy` without a domain terminal. A malformed
policy object is distinct from absent, stale, or incompatible policy activation
evidence, which produces a fail-closed unavailable terminal for an admitted
obligation.

### Run context and policy activation

Actual mode is not accepted from the policy or target. Optional immutable
`RunRiskContext` evidence contains:

- run-manifest integrity ID and run ID;
- actual `contracts::RunMode`;
- replay class and replay-evidence ID;
- manifest/configuration epoch;
- selected effective run-input sequence; and
- quality status.

Optional immutable `RiskPolicyActivation` evidence contains:

- accepted `run.control.*` outcome event ID;
- run ID, risk-scope ID, and configuration epoch;
- the exact risk-policy, limit-set, exposure-model, arithmetic, and authority
  versions activated;
- activation effective sequence and logical time; and
- quality status.

Both must be present, quality-valid, exact-scope, no newer than the evaluation
cut, and mutually compatible. The policy's supported mode must equal the
manifest mode. Missing, stale, future, or incompatible evidence produces
`RiskObligationUnavailable`. A valid `live_read_only` or `capture` manifest is
an admission rejection because those modes cannot admit authoritative risk.

### Publication and admission evidence

`TargetAdmissionEvidence` retains three independent facts:

- optional `TargetPublicationFact`: publication-attempt ID, target ID,
  published/not-published result, effective sequence, and logical time;
- optional `TargetAcknowledgementFact`: consumer-boundary ID, publication
  attempt ID, target ID, acknowledged/not-acknowledged result, effective
  sequence, and logical time; and
- optional `TargetLifecycleFact`: lifecycle event ID, target ID, active,
  superseded, or invalidated state, effective sequence, and logical time.

Every fact is evaluated as of the selected cut. The acknowledgement must refer
to the selected successful publication. Independent identities and positions
preserve the history of a target that was published and acknowledged and later
superseded. Missing, negative, future, mismatched, superseded, or invalidated
facts produce their own admission reasons.

Admission also validates exact target/policy scope, run, scale, target validity,
non-future cut, and any known manifest mode. `capture` and `live_read_only` are
not authoritative risk modes. A known admission failure produces
`RiskAdmissionRejected`, not a `RiskDecision` and not an accepted obligation
ID. Missing run or policy evidence is handled after known target admission
checks as an unavailable accepted obligation.

### Required safety evidence

The evaluation context contains optional immutable snapshots:

- `AccountRiskSnapshot`: state-view ID, run/account/portfolio scope, signed
  position, available capital, scale, quality, sequence, logical time, and
  account-trading-enabled state;
- `MarketRiskSnapshot`: state-view ID, run/instrument/listing scope, quality,
  sequence, logical time, and tradeable state;
- `ProjectedExposureSnapshot`: projected-exposure ID, risk scope, target key,
  signed worst-case exposure before this target, scale, `risk_sequence`,
  quality, sequence, and logical time; and
- `KillSwitchSnapshot`: state-view ID, risk scope, quality, sequence, logical
  time, and enabled state.

Each present snapshot must be quality-valid, scope-compatible, same-scale where
amounts apply, no newer than the evaluation cut, and within the policy age.
Absence and each invalidity map to a stable unavailable reason. The fixed
precedence is run context, policy activation, account, market, projected
exposure, then kill switch.

The account snapshot is the authoritative current position for this evaluation.
The current exposure retained in M6.1's target is lineage only and may be older.
The `QuantityOnlyV1` exposure model computes, with checked signed arithmetic:

```text
requested_delta = requested_target - account_current_position
requested_projected_exposure =
    projected_exposure_before_target + requested_delta
```

The policy limit applies to `abs(requested_projected_exposure)`, not merely to
the target amount. The computed delta and requested projected exposure are
recorded in every semantic decision. Overflow or an unrepresentable absolute
value produces `RiskObligationUnavailable::ArithmeticUnrepresentable`.

### Semantic decision

`RiskDecision` records:

- decision, obligation, and terminal outcome IDs;
- approved, modified, or rejected disposition;
- requested target identity and complete target key;
- authoritative account current amount, requested target amount, requested
  delta, projected exposure before target, requested projected exposure, and
  optional authorized target/delta/projected exposure;
- all policy versions and risk scope;
- run-manifest, policy-activation, account, market, projected-exposure, and
  kill-switch evidence identities;
- evaluated `risk_sequence`;
- issue cut, logical expiry, and run mode;
- stable binding reason and deterministic ordered rule findings; and
- an optional `QuantityOnlyRiskReductionProof` for modification.

Known safety states are semantic policy results, not unavailable evidence:

- enabled kill switch rejects;
- disabled account trading rejects;
- non-tradeable market rejects;
- an in-limit requested projected exposure approves;
- an over-limit requested projected exposure modifies when modification is
  enabled and the solved target passes the complete reduction proof;
- otherwise an over-limit target rejects.

The authority evaluates all known safety rules in fixed order and records every
triggered rejection rule. The binding reason is the first rule in policy order:
kill switch, account disabled, market non-tradeable, then exposure limit.

### Modification proof

The only M6.2 modification clamps the requested projected exposure to the
nearest same-sign boundary `[-limit, +limit]`, then solves the authorized
absolute target from the exposure model:

```text
authorized_projected_exposure = clamp(requested_projected_exposure)
authorized_delta =
    authorized_projected_exposure - projected_exposure_before_target
authorized_target = account_current_position + authorized_delta
```

`QuantityOnlyRiskReductionProof` records requested and authorized target,
delta, and projected-exposure absolute values, the limit, and the
`QuantityOnlyV1` dimension declaration.

Modification is valid only when:

- all values use the same target key and scale;
- authorized absolute projected exposure is strictly lower than requested and
  no greater than the limit;
- authorized movement `abs(authorized_delta)` is no greater than
  `abs(requested_delta)`;
- authorized movement has the same sign as requested movement or is zero;
- the authorized target differs from account current position; and
- all arithmetic is representable.

If solving the clamp violates any proof condition, or would produce no movement,
the decision is rejected. `QuantityOnlyV1` declares quantity exposure and target
movement as the complete relevant risk dimensions for this policy version.
Notional, leverage, concentration, liquidity sizing, multi-leg, or conversion
requirements are unsupported target schemas and cannot be admitted under this
policy; they are not silently treated as irrelevant. Adding one requires a new
exposure model and policy version.

### Unavailable and admission outcomes

`RiskObligationUnavailable` records deterministic obligation/outcome identity,
target and scope, policy versions, cut, the first unavailable reason, and every
available evidence identity. It has no decision ID and no authorized target.

`RiskAdmissionRejected` records deterministic outcome identity, target and
policy scope, cut, publication/acknowledgement/lifecycle fact references, and
the admission reason. It has no obligation ID, no decision ID, and no
authorized target.

`RiskDecision::authorizes_target()` is a static semantic predicate that is true
only for approved and modified decisions. It does not claim current reservation
eligibility. Rejected decisions, unavailable obligations, and admission
rejections do not authorize a target. M6.3 must separately check decision
expiry, current target lifecycle, projected-exposure precondition, deduplication,
and capacity before reservation. Every output remains non-executable.

Decision expiry is `min(cut logical time + policy decision duration, source
target validity end)`, using checked arithmetic. A decision can never outlive
its source target.

## Deterministic Evaluation Order

1. Validate policy configuration.
2. Validate independent publication, acknowledgement, lifecycle, target/policy
   scope, any present manifest mode, cut, and target expiry; emit admission
   rejection on the first known failure.
3. Derive the risk-obligation ID from admitted target, policy, publication, and
   cut.
4. Check run-context, policy-activation, and safety-evidence presence in fixed
   order.
5. Check quality, activation, scope, scale, future sequence/time, and age in
   fixed order.
6. If any evidence is unavailable, emit one unavailable terminal with the first
   reason and no decision.
7. Compute requested delta and requested projected exposure with checked
   arithmetic.
8. Evaluate all known semantic safety rules in fixed order.
9. Reject when any safety rule triggers; otherwise approve, modify with proof,
   or reject the over-limit target under policy.
10. Derive complete terminal identity from all semantic fields and evidence.

No iteration over unordered storage participates in evaluation or identity.
Checked integer arithmetic handles absolute values, age, expiry, and clamping;
`INT64_MIN` fails closed as unavailable arithmetic evidence.

## Reason Taxonomy

Admission reasons distinguish missing/negative/future publication,
acknowledgement and lifecycle facts; mismatched fact identities;
superseded/invalidated target; scope/run/scale mismatch; future cut; expired
target; unsupported target schema; and inadmissible known mode.

Unavailable reasons distinguish missing, non-valid quality, scope/version/
activation mismatch, scale mismatch, future evidence, stale evidence, and
arithmetic inability for run context, policy activation, and each required
snapshot family.

Decision reasons are `WithinPermissiveLimit`, `KillSwitchEnabled`,
`AccountTradingDisabled`, `MarketNotTradeable`,
`ClampedToProjectedExposureLimit`, `ProjectedExposureLimitExceeded`, and
`ModifiedTargetWouldNotChangeExposure`.

Enums are closed and invalid enum values fail safely. Human-readable text is a
projection of stable codes and does not enter identity.

## Testing

The C++ matrix covers:

- valid approval with authoritative run-manifest and policy-activation lineage;
- sign-preserving positive and negative modifications and reduction proofs;
- modification-disabled and no-change-after-clamp rejections;
- simultaneous known safety triggers and deterministic binding precedence;
- every independent publication, acknowledgement, lifecycle, and admission
  reason at before/at/after-cut positions;
- missing, stale, gapped, recovering, invalid, unavailable, future,
  wrong-scope, and wrong-scale evidence for every snapshot family;
- target/policy/evidence boundary values, `INT64_MIN`, age and expiry overflow;
- deterministic IDs, semantic sensitivity, repeatability, and independence
  from irrelevant object/call order;
- `authorizes_target()` only for approved/modified decisions, with expiry no
  later than target expiry;
- no executable output and no reservation or `risk_sequence` mutation; and
- invalid policy and invalid enum values.

Python/CMake tests prove owner registration, exact target graph, allowed
backward dependency on portfolio only, forbidden downstream includes/links,
guard execution, include-module/dynamic/deferred mutation resistance, and
compatibility with the existing M5 and M6.1 gates.

Full acceptance remains `make m0-check` and `make cpp-profiles-check`.

## Deferred Scope

M6.3 owns current reservation eligibility, projected-exposure mutation,
single-writer serialization,
reservation request/outcome, capacity contention, expiry/release, and sequence
advancement. M6.4 owns executable paper intent. Paper fills, ledger, positions,
P&L, durable interruption/recovery and Phase 03 run-scope failure recording,
live human approval, cross-target allocation, multi-leg
risk, capital formulas, liquidity sizing, and production limits remain later
deliverables.
